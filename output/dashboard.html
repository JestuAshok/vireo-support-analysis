6. Known Limitations & Methodological Caveats (from NOTES.md) Audit Log
1. Refund-Notes Exposure (Rs 19 – 36 Lakh) Approximately 1,000 tickets have agent notes recording refund initiation while refund_amount_inr is blank. Valuation indicates an estimated rupee exposure of Rs 19.2–36.0 Lakh (Mid-Case: Rs 31.6 Lakh). Crucially, audit confirms these represent customers' own paid money (order cancellations, delivery failures, duplicate charges, return QC delays) rather than vendor replacement losses.
2. Bot Category Tagging Errors (13-20% in a 30-ticket read) Audit of ticket text reveals that bot-assigned categories misclassify 13-20% in a 30-ticket read (e.g. charging and hardware failures mislabelled as 'Audio Quality', or delivery transit failures tagged as 'Other').
3. Structured vs. Text Action Discrepancies 12.3% of tickets (1,445 / 11,750) note replacements dispatched in agent notes while replacement_issued == 'N'. Conversely, 8.9% note refunds while monetary fields are blank.
4. Lot Code Absence in Free-Text Lot and batch numbers appear in 0.00% of customer messages or agent notes (0/2,571 tickets). Lot identification is discoverable strictly via structured order linkages.
5. Non-Comparability of Tier 2 Tier 2 handles multi-day escalations (median handle time 3.1 to 5.1 days vs. 20–27 minutes for Tier 1 frontline chat). Comparing Tier 2 agents against frontline baselines violates policy and operational reality.
6. Chronological Order Date Nuance In 175 tickets joined by order_id, order_date > created_at (primarily pilot/seeding units or system logging quirks). Excluding them changes group replacement rates by ≤ 0.30 pts and CSAT by ≤ 0.014 pts, well below significant thresholds.
